Methodology

How the numbers are calculated, where the data comes from, and what it can't tell you.

Calculation

Every figure is hours of work = cost ÷ hourly wage.

  • Wage used. If you enter a wage, that is used. Annual pay is divided by 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). If you leave it blank, the metro's median hourly wage for all occupations is used.
  • Rent: monthly median gross rent ÷ wage = hours of work per month.
  • Gas, per gallon: price per gallon ÷ wage.
  • Gas, fill-up: 15 gallons × price per gallon ÷ wage. The 15-gallon size is fixed.
  • Groceries, each item: estimated local price ÷ wage. The estimated local price is the national average price × the metro's price level for all goods ÷ 100 (see Value types).
  • Groceries, whole basket: one of each of the six items (1 gallon of milk, 1 dozen eggs, and 1 lb each of bread, ground beef, bananas, and coffee), added up, ÷ wage. This is a small sample of staples, not a grocery budget.
  • Owning a home, typical costs: median monthly costs for owners with a mortgage ÷ wage = hours of work per month.
  • Owning a home, mortgage payment: monthly principal and interest on the metro's median home value, ÷ wage. Loan = home value × (1 − down payment). Payment = loan × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n), where i is the annual rate ÷ 12 and n is the number of monthly payments. You enter the rate; the down payment starts at 20% and the term at 30 years, and you can change both. Property taxes and homeowner's insurance are not included.
  • Display. Results under one hour are shown in whole minutes; otherwise in hours to one decimal place.

Value types

Every number on the site carries a label saying what kind of value it is. Select “How is this calculated?” beside any figure to see its method, inputs, and as-of date.

  • Observed Published directly by a government agency for that metro and period: wages, rent, gas, home values, and owner costs.
  • Estimated A national figure adjusted by a published local price index. Local grocery prices are estimated: no public source publishes grocery prices for every metro, so the national average price from BLS is multiplied by the metro's Regional Price Parity for all goods from BEA (U.S. = 100). BEA publishes no food-specific index, so the all-goods index is an approximation for grocery prices. These are not prices observed in the metro.
  • Scenario Calculated from stated assumptions, which are always shown beside the result: the whole grocery basket (it depends on the chosen quantities) and the mortgage payment (it depends on the rate, down payment, and term).

Missing data

Missing values are shown as missing. They are never filled in from another year, a nearby area, or a state or regional average. Estimated values are different: they are calculated by a stated method for every metro the inputs cover, and they are always labeled Estimated.

  • No data: the source publishes no estimate for that metro (for example, too few survey responses).
  • Not covered: the source doesn't cover that metro at all. This is the case for gas in all but 10 metros, and for grocery estimates in the 6 Puerto Rico metros, which have no BEA price level.
  • Top-coded: the source reports only a range, such as rent of “$3,500 or more”. owner costs of “$4,000 or more”, or a home value of “$2,000,000 or more”. Hours aren't calculated from a range, and no mortgage payment is calculated from a top-coded home value.

Limitations

Wages

  • Pre-tax. Wages are before taxes and deductions, so take-home pay buys less than shown.
  • A median isn't your pay. Half of workers in a metro earn less than the median and half earn more. The median covers all occupations combined.
  • Lag. OEWS is published once a year, about a year after its reference period.
  • Annual conversion. Dividing annual pay by 2,080 assumes full-time, year-round work with no overtime.

Rent

  • Survey estimates. ACS figures come from a sample and carry margins of error (shown as ±, at 90% confidence). Smaller metros have larger margins.
  • All renters, not new leases. The median includes long-time tenants, so it is usually below what a new renter would be asked to pay today.
  • Includes utilities. Gross rent is contract rent plus utilities and fuels paid by the renter.

Gas

  • Only 10 metros. EIA publishes city prices for only 10 areas. Every other metro shows no gas data.
  • Boundaries may differ. EIA's city sampling areas may not match metro boundaries exactly.
  • Regular grade, one month. Prices are for regular gasoline, averaged over a single month, and change month to month.

Groceries

  • Estimates, not local prices. Local prices are the national average scaled by an all-goods price level. Food prices can differ from the general price level in a metro.
  • Different years. National prices are from the latest month; the newest BEA price levels are for ….
  • Six items. The basket is a small sample of staples, one of each, not a grocery budget or a nutritional plan.

Owning a home

  • Typical owner costs include more than the mortgage. They include property taxes, insurance, utilities, and fees, and are for all owners with a mortgage, including those who bought years ago at different prices and rates.
  • The mortgage payment is a scenario. It uses the rate you enter (the site doesn't supply one yet), and covers principal and interest only. It leaves out property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and mortgage insurance, so a real payment would be higher.
  • Median home value, not a listing price. The ACS asks owners what their home is worth; homes for sale today may cost more or less.
  • Survey estimates. Like rent, these come from the ACS sample and carry margins of error.

Across sources

  • Different periods. …. Gas is the most recent, so it is compared with older wages. If prices have risen since the wage period, hours are somewhat overstated.
  • One number per metro. Metros include cities, suburbs, and rural counties; costs vary within a metro.
  • Limited costs. Only rent, gas, six grocery staples, and homeownership are covered. Dining out, transportation, childcare, healthcare, and other costs are not included, and the figures are not added into a total.

Privacy

If you enter a wage or mortgage details, they stay in your browser tab. It is never saved (no cookies or local storage), never sent to a server, and never placed in the page address. The site has no accounts and no analytics.
