Methodology

How the numbers are calculated, where the data comes from, and what it can't tell you.

Calculation · Sources · Missing data · Limitations · Privacy · Attribution

Calculation

Every figure is hours of work = cost ÷ hourly wage.

  • Wage used. If you enter a wage, that is used. Annual pay is divided by 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). If you leave it blank, the metro's median hourly wage for all occupations is used.
  • Rent: monthly median gross rent ÷ wage = hours of work per month.
  • Gas, per gallon: price per gallon ÷ wage.
  • Gas, fill-up: 15 gallons × price per gallon ÷ wage. The 15-gallon size is fixed.
  • Display. Results under one hour are shown in whole minutes; otherwise in hours to one decimal place.

Missing data

Missing values are shown as missing. They are never estimated or filled in from another year, a nearby area, or a state or regional average.

  • No data: the source publishes no estimate for that metro (for example, too few survey responses).
  • Not covered: the source doesn't cover that metro at all. This is the case for gas in all but 10 metros.
  • Top-coded: the source reports only a range, such as rent of “$3,500 or more”. Hours aren't calculated from a range.

Limitations

Wages

  • Pre-tax. Wages are before taxes and deductions, so take-home pay buys less than shown.
  • A median isn't your pay. Half of workers in a metro earn less than the median and half earn more. The median covers all occupations combined.
  • Lag. OEWS is published once a year, about a year after its reference period.
  • Annual conversion. Dividing annual pay by 2,080 assumes full-time, year-round work with no overtime.

Rent

  • Survey estimates. ACS figures come from a sample and carry margins of error (shown as ±, at 90% confidence). Smaller metros have larger margins.
  • All renters, not new leases. The median includes long-time tenants, so it is usually below what a new renter would be asked to pay today.
  • Includes utilities. Gross rent is contract rent plus utilities and fuels paid by the renter.

Gas

  • Only 10 metros. EIA publishes city prices for only 10 areas. Every other metro shows no gas data.
  • Boundaries may differ. EIA's city sampling areas may not match metro boundaries exactly.
  • Regular grade, one month. Prices are for regular gasoline, averaged over a single month, and change month to month.

Across sources

  • Different periods. …. Gas is the most recent, so it is compared with older wages. If prices have risen since the wage period, hours are somewhat overstated.
  • One number per metro. Metros include cities, suburbs, and rural counties; costs vary within a metro.
  • Limited costs. Only rent and gas are covered. Groceries, dining out, transportation, childcare, healthcare, and other costs are not included yet; groceries and dining out lack good metro-level public sources.

Privacy

If you enter a wage, it stays in your browser tab. It is never saved (no cookies or local storage), never sent to a server, and never placed in the page address. The site has no accounts and no analytics.
